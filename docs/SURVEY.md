# Survey: K and L against the rules, the crowd, prior art and past winners

Written Thu 2026-11-12 afternoon (Cairo), read-only research. Sources are linked inline; rule citations are pages
of the Official Rules PDF (English, last updated Nov 5, 2026, 16 pages; it is the newest version in Epic's rules folder).

## Summary

**K. Meanwhile...: go.** It breaks no rule. None of the seven projects found so far does anything like it. The
nearest older games (Night Trap and Double Switch, Five Nights at Freddy's, Mini Metro) are far enough away, and
they offer three cheap fixes for the "babysitting timers" risk: show every room's danger at once, keep runs short,
and escalate. Feel is still the open question, and only playing it answers that.

**L. The Tight Edit: go, with a caveat.** It breaks no rule. But the only entry found that overlaps either candidate
overlaps L: *FINAL CUT* (solo developer plus Claude) puts shot cards on a timeline, you press PLAY, and actors on a
miniature set act them out. That is the "editing puzzle" the brainstorm crossed out as crowd idea 3, and it is L's
surface. Framed and Timelie show the edit-then-watch loop works, but reviewers call it finicky and short. L stands
apart only if the stunt and the near-miss score are what a judge sees first, not the timeline. On a tie, the case
for K is now stronger.

**Rule risks, most important first:**
1. **Sourced assets aren't scored** (p11, 15(c)). Anything you didn't make yourself, such as Fab packs or
   WeLoveIndies sounds, is "not reviewed or scored". AI-generated content is *not* a sourced asset, so it counts as
   your own work. Read literally, a game whose sound is all library audio earns nothing for it under Audio.
2. **A Windows package is mandatory** for UE entries (p1, 1(b)). Web appears only on the jam page, as experimental.
   Never ship Web alone.
3. **Student category:** every team member must be "currently enrolled at a higher-educational institution" (p1,
   p3). The rules name no proof; Epic verifies at prize time (p4 7(j), p9 §14). Recruiting one non-student
   teammate gives up the category.
4. **Egypt is eligible.** It isn't on the restricted list (p1). You need to be 18+, have a registered address there,
   and have a non-cabined Epic account (p3). Not verified: prize payout through Hyperwallet with a W-8BEN form (p8,
   p10) under the catch-all clause about places "where we cannot distribute prizes" (p1).
5. **Smaller rules that bind:**
   - a person, not an agent, presses Submit (p12 §17);
   - no trademarks or logos except the UE logo (p11 15(d));
   - content suitable for Teen (p5);
   - the video must be 1920x1080 or higher and in English (p4 7(c), 7(i));
   - the dev log must be submitted with the entry for Glass Box (p7);
   - a team can win at most one main prize and one modifier (p7, p9).

**Where JAM_PLAN.md is wrong or incomplete:**
- *Hyper3D pool:* the plan reads it as a prize for using Rodin. The jam page says the $10,000 is split among
  **winning** teams that used Rodin, so it only pays if you win something else.
- *Assets:* "fine with a licence" is true, but they don't score (above).
- *Prize targets:* Render Royalty ($2,500, best visuals; the NVIDIA RTX award sits inside it) is missing. You can
  win only one modifier, so Solo Driver and Glass Box can't both be won (p7).
- *Video:* the 1920x1080 minimum is missing (p4 7(c)).
- *Criteria name:* the rules say "Level Design" (p6); the jam page says "Level design and game progression".
  Harmless.
- *Pre-start declaration:* the Official Rules don't require one; the jam page FAQ does. The rules add that Epic may
  ask for git history, so never rewrite or squash history.

**What this changes for K and L:**
- Neither breaks a rule.
- Both: no real studio logos, film-stock brands or MPAA rating marks on title cards (M's "PG rating" would need a
  made-up rating).
- K's fights stay Teen-level: no gore.
- Both should get characters and props from Tripo, Rodin or PBRgen rather than Fab packs, because those count as
  your own work and score.
- Plan some audio that is made here (generated or recorded) as well as WeLoveIndies, or Audio may score on nothing.

## 3. Prior art

### K: cutting between characters in danger; plate-spinning under a timer

| Game | Core loop | What players and critics said | Take for the first 30 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Night Trap** (1992) / **Double Switch** (1993) | Switch between live camera feeds of rooms and spring traps at the right moment ([reviews](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2017/08/23/night-trap-25th-anniversary-edition-review/), [Hardcore Gaming 101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/double-switch/)) | The camera-hopping concept is remembered fondly. Tedium came from memorising fixed timings and replaying long stretches after one mistake. The sequel added a mini-map, and the 2017 remaster added thumbnails of every feed, so you stop channel-hopping blind. | Show every off-screen room's danger at all times; K's small monitors are exactly that fix. Keep spawns systemic, not scripted, so nobody has to memorise anything. |
| **Five Nights at Freddy's** (2014) | Check cameras while threats advance in the rooms you aren't watching; a power meter limits you ([GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/five-nights-at-freddys)) | The tension of not looking is praised. Critics say it becomes repetitive once mastered and leans on jump scares; most of its tricks are out by night 3. | The first threat arrives slowly with an unmistakable sound cue. Plan escalation (a third hero, faster hordes) before the loop is learned. |
| **Mini Metro** (2015; from memory, not re-checked) | Keep many stations flowing; an overcrowded station shows a filling ring, and one full ring ends the run | Praised as calm but readable overload, where you lose to a visible, fair timer | Put the danger timer *on* the thing in danger, and make the first failure take long enough to learn from. |
| **Multitask** (IcyLime, Flash) | Several tiny games at once on a split screen, added one by one ([Gamezebo](https://www.gamezebo.com/online-games/multitask), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/multitask)) | Starts with one simple task, then the screen splits; known for quick overload and short runs | Start with one hero. Open the second room only after a few seconds of success. |

**Too close?** No. The nearest is Night Trap, but K is real-time action on screen with a reward for cutting late; Night
Trap is timed trap-springing on video. Naming note: *Meanwhile* is also the title of Jason Shiga's well-known
branching comic (from memory). That's fine as a working title; check before it becomes the final name.

**What this changes for K:**
- Danger for every off-screen room on screen at once.
- Short runs with instant retry.
- One hero at the start, the second room opening after about 10 s.
- Escalation planned before the fun gate.

### L: arranging timed events on a timeline; edit-then-watch; near-miss scoring

| Game | Core loop | What players and critics said | Take for the first 30 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Framed** (2014) | Rearrange comic panels so the noir hero escapes; watch the result ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/framed/review/), [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/review-framed-collection/)) | Art, jazz and animation praised, Metacritic 85. Complaints: very short, the novelty wears thin, no instructions, and the timing puzzles are stressful. Failures were "almost worth it" for the animations. | Make a failed take fun to watch: ragdoll plus "Cut! Medic!". Failure should be a reward, not a punishment. |
| **Timelie** (2020) | Scrub a timeline to plan a girl's and a cat's moves past sentries ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/timelie-review), [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/9506/timelie/reviews)) | The mechanic is called one of a kind. Complaints: finicky solutions, slow pace, repetitive trial and error, late levels "cool but not fun". | Show trap timings on the timeline before play. Keep each level to one idea and a few seconds of play. |
| **Opus Magnum** (2017) | Build a machine, watch it run, then optimise cost, cycles and area against histograms of other players ([fan analysis](https://biggieblog.com/tracking-the-global-opus-magnum-records/)) | Comparison histograms and friend leaderboards are what drive players back to re-solve finished levels | Right after the first survival, show the margin and a target next to it ("Director's Cut: every miss under 0.25 s"). That is L's whole pass condition. |
| **SUPERHOT** (2016; from memory) | Plan-like play, then a real-time replay of your run as one continuous film | The replay is the payoff players remember | After a clean take, replay it at full speed with cuts. That is the stunt reel and the trailer. |

**Too close?** No single game. But the combination (Framed's order puzzle, Timelie's timeline, and FINAL CUT in this
jam) means a judge's first glance at a timeline under a 3D set reads as familiar. L is distinct only in continuous
timing, trimming and near-miss scoring.

**What this changes for L:**
- In the grey box, test whether shaving margins is fun, not whether arranging is.
- If the pass ("re-edit to shave without being asked") only happens because of the readout, keep the readout
  prominent from the first level.

**Patterns:**
- **The theme is taken literally, but as a mechanic.** 2025's winners turned "Here We Go Again" into a future self
  and death silhouettes, and the student winner is the retake-with-ghosts idea BRAINSTORM crossed out as crowd
  idea 2, done well. The crowd idea wasn't punished; mediocre execution was.
- **Small premises win:** a ghost moving lamps took 2nd place. Teams of 4-5 took UE 1st and Student 1st; a solo
  entry won Army of One (Memory Gate).
- **Stylized looks win:** stylized and photoreal had separate visuals awards in past years. This year there is one
  award, Render Royalty, and it covers both.
- **Judging is slow and done by people:** in 2025 a panel of industry specialists played every entry, took notes
  and narrowed to finalists, and results took ~5 months
  ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/2025-epic-megajam-mega-stack-o-jam-update/2689696)). This year has
  4 days instead of ~7, and 8,570 sign-ups against 490 entries last year. Inference: expect a skim-first funnel
  where the video and the first minute decide who gets a full play.

**What the organisers have said** (jam page and rules; the kickoff stream itself wasn't watched, and no transcript
was found):
- *Ambition:* something you couldn't usually pull off in four days: a new mechanic, a bold art style, or a bigger
  scope.
- *Theme:* a fresh, clever take.
- *Gameplay:* solid, original mechanics that feel good.
- *Level design:* spaces that support the gameplay and guide the player.
- *Tips:* scope small, then polish; a finished, fun game beats an ambitious broken one; show gameplay in the first
  five seconds of the video; package early.
- *Rules:* ties are broken on Fun counted twice; AI use doesn't change the score.

**What this changes for K and L:**
- Both are mechanical readings of "edit", which matches what has won.
- L names the theme more directly (Studio Cut and Director's Cut medals) and should score higher on Theme.
- K is likelier to be fun within the first minute, which the bigger field and the Fun tie-break reward.
- Either way, put more effort into the video's first 5 s and the game's first minute than into extra levels.

## The three findings that matter most

1. **Third-party assets aren't scored; AI-generated assets count as yours** (p11, 15(c)). Make characters, props,
   materials and some of the audio with the AI tools, not from Fab or library packs, or Visuals and Audio have
   nothing to score.
2. **L's surface is already in the field; K's isn't.** FINAL CUT (solo developer plus Claude) is timeline, press
   play, miniature set. If the grey boxes tie, the tie-break toward K now has evidence behind it.
3. **Winners use the theme as a literal mechanic, and this field is ~17x bigger than 2025's.** The first 5 s of
   video and the first minute of play decide whether a judge gets further.
